## Goal

Independently diagnose the 38 accepted financial-reconciliation scenarios, trace the 11 previously alleged implementation gaps, and separate source defects from verification gaps. This is not authorization to change financial behavior or access a database.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [x] Trace each alleged gap and classify all 38 scenarios against the accepted scenario and accounting contracts.
- [x] Distinguish source, offline tests, current and historical runtime evidence, application boundary, and independent acceptance.
- [x] Investigate the supplier timeout without inventing its cause; identify root causes and a dependency-ordered plan.
- [x] Run focused offline checks and audit worktree preservation.

## Out of Scope

Database connections and runtime tests, implementation, schema or privilege changes, accounting-policy changes, recovery, deployment, and acceptance of financial correctness.

## Authority and methodology

The [decision log](./financial-reconciliation-decision-log.md), [policy matrix](./financial-reconciliation-policy-matrix.md), [invariants](./financial-reconciliation-accounting-invariants.md), and [scenario matrix](./financial-reconciliation-scenario-matrix.md) are the approved requirements. The [decision pack](./financial-reconciliation-accounting-decision-pack.md), [architecture](./financial-reconciliation-architecture.md), [closure report](./financial-reconciliation-closure-report.md), [gap analysis](./financial-reconciliation-gap-analysis.md), and [test report](./financial-reconciliation-test-report.md) describe earlier states; their pending-approval status and claims that allocation/refunds do not exist are superseded by later decisions and current code. The [engineering plan](./financial-reconciliation-engineering-plan.md) records intended boundaries, not evidence that they work. The previous [classification](./scenario-evidence-classification-2026-11-09.md) is a lead, not an authority.

Money definitions: gross AR/AP is invoice exposure after valid allocations and approved adjustments; usable payment is source amount minus active allocations, completed refunds and payment reversal; unapplied credit/advance is the relevant usable amount; net is gross less unapplied. Allocation reversal restores usable credit; it is not also a refund. `refundable` can exceed `usable` when an allocation can first be reversed. Rejected validation cases produce no financial write. The approved lock protocol requires common ordered parents, post-lock reread and atomic/idempotent mutation; policy approval itself is not proof.

Application boundary means a guarded server action and its caller, not simply an exported service. A search of `src` found no callers of the [reconciliation actions](../../../src/app/_actions/reconciliation.actions.ts) beyond their definitions. Payment creation is connected to the [customer](../../../src/app/billing/payments/new/_form.tsx) and [supplier](../../../src/app/purchases/payments/new/_form.tsx) forms. Thus many historical service/action fixtures are meaningful but do not prove an operator can perform the complete workflow in the product. This is an application integration finding, not a claim that the server actions cannot be invoked.

## S-27 decision boundary

S-27 is not among the 13 later scenario resolutions in the [decision log](./financial-reconciliation-decision-log.md). Decision O approves rejection of unknown methods, not rejection of Cheque. Both customer and supplier forms intentionally offer Cheque, validation accepts it and it persists as `CHEQUE`; unknown strings throw rather than map to `CREDIT`. No inspected evidence establishes deprecation. Decision needed: **keep Cheque and change the rejection fixture to an unknown value, or deliberately disallow Cheque and specify treatment of existing Cheque records**. This question does not reopen Decisions A–O or any of the 13 approved resolutions.

## Source-hash applicability, with actual changes

The [B](./replay5d1b46962e3d-campaign.json), [T](./replay90e2ad24bdf3-campaign.json), and [O](./replay0a68a1e4655e-campaign.json) artifacts record the same older schema and reconciliation-service hashes. Matching byte-for-byte copies were located in this session's `receipt-policy-baseline` and compared with current files:

1. The reconciliation service's **only textual change** is retry maximum **3→5** and the corresponding exhaustion condition. Normal successful allocation/refund/reversal arithmetic and ledger code are textually unchanged; retry/concurrency and safe-exhaustion behavior are directly affected for all B/T/O reconciliation-service scenarios.
2. The schema diff adds the receipt-sequence tenant relation and `next_value` default. This is relevant to receipt provisioning and payment creation, but is not itself a change to the allocation/refund arithmetic. The historical and current 0027 refund migration snapshot is identical.
3. T's hash-matched historical harness differs from current; B/O exact matching harness snapshots were not located. The artifacts' captured fields are not a complete transitive dependency inventory. E/M compare equal for all hashes those campaigns recorded; that does not prove unrecorded dependencies.

Thus B's S-02/03/05/06/11–15/17/29/34–36, T's S-16, and O's S-08 remain **valid historical observations**, with particularly relevant functional evidence for ordinary paths. They cannot establish final-source runtime acceptance because retry behavior and other tested inputs/harness context differ or were not fully bound; this is not evidence that ordinary arithmetic regressed. E/M PASS is limited to S-01/04/28. No historical status is overwritten.

[M campaign](./replaycab6b49cae03-campaign.json) records the receipt suite PASS, then `spawnSync ... ETIMEDOUT` at 240 seconds; no full-harness result artifact exists. The [driver](../../../scripts/financial-reconciliation-runtime-harness.cjs#L1210) invokes a sequential full suite before supplier allocation/refund/reversal sections, and only writes the `results` array near the end. The session's final-replay driver used a 240,000 ms process timeout and throws `result.error` before recording stdout/stderr. Therefore the exact active operation, barrier and assertion progress cannot be determined. The harness has receipt and allocation barriers and awaits `runtime.end()` / `bootstrap.end()` without its own explicit per-operation or cleanup deadlines; this establishes possible unobservability, **not** that any of those waits caused this timeout. Prisma transaction defaults are not treated here as a demonstrated operation deadline for all calls.

Existing selection only handles classification, classification-refund and receipt; supplier cache assertions are embedded in the full flow, with shared fixture state. They can be isolated by carefully constructing a dedicated guarded fixture/selection in a later authorized tooling task and emitting sanitized per-assertion progress, without changing financial logic. Add bounded diagnostic deadlines with explicit timeout outcomes and safe cleanup; do not merely increase the global timeout. No assertion may be called PASS or FAIL from this timeout.
